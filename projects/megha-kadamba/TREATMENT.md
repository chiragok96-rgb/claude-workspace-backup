# Megha Kadamba (ମେଘ କଦମ୍ବ): treatment

**Brief.** A lyric music video for the user's Odia romantic song clip (32.2 s, about 117.5 BPM). The song is the only audio: no voice-over, no added music and no foley. Vertical 1080×1920 at 30 fps. Three library styles carry one warm palette. The lyrics are burned in, in Odia.

## Three candidate structures

1. **One lamp, three media.** The film starts as a paper-cut lightbox, which is the verse's wonder ("which Konark are you?"). The light becomes paper, and the paper becomes watercolour (alta red blooming on the ground, spring). At the chorus the wet red thickens into palette-knife oil paint, under monsoon clouds and a kadamba tree. Each medium is "heavier" than the one before, as the singer's longing grows. **← chosen.**
2. **Four seasons of one face.** A single locked portrait, repainted in a different medium each section. Rejected: the lyrics are about places and nature as metaphors (Konark, alta, Phagun, cloud and kadamba), not a face, and a locked frame wastes the chorus lift.
3. **A journey across Odisha.** A lateral travel from the Konark coast to a monsoon grove. Rejected: it's a travelogue, and a 32 s clip has room for metaphors, not geography.

**Why the first.** The song's own images are its four metaphors. Moving between media at the musical seams (7.87 s and 15.88 s) lets each section feel new while the shared vermilion and gold palette keeps it one film. The chorus lift at 15.88 s is where the native move lands.

## Logline and arc

A lover compares his beloved to a temple, a spring and a monsoon flower. The more he tries to name her, the richer the paint becomes.
- **Setup** (0–7.9 s): darkness; a single sun-hole of light opens; paper layers light up; her silhouette, then the Konark wheel.
- **Turn** (7.9–15.9 s): light becomes paper; alta-red footprints paint the earth into hibiscus red; a spring flame-tree paints itself in; a breath at about 14.5 s.
- **Ending** (15.9–32.2 s): red pigment thickens into knife paint; monsoon clouds and the golden kadamba; rain; the kadamba blossom's round becomes the sun wheel again, which echoes the opening.

## Benchmarks

- **Classical Odissi dance photography and pattachitra scroll framing.** Learn: profile silhouettes, ornament read as shape, and arched frames within frames. Don't take: any specific painting, dancer or photograph.
- **Hand-made Indian monsoon-song cinematography** (rain on leaves, a golden hour under clouds). Learn: slow push-ins, light breaking through cloud edges, and holding on a single flower. Don't take: shots, characters or a specific film.

## Sound design

The song only. No ambience, foley or score is added. Picture events are locked to the song's beats instead. `mux.sh` masters it to −14 LUFS.

## Subtitles and titles

- Lightbox section: the lyric is a cut paper strip on the dark front band, growing from its left edge, warm off-white with a soft shadow, in Noto Serif Oriya.
- Watercolor section: ink-brown Odia text over a paper halo, revealed left to right like writing, in Noto Serif Oriya.
- Impasto section: crisp cream serif text on a calm dark plane, in Baloo Bhaina 2 semibold for weight against the knife paint.
- The type sits in the lower third (at about y 1440–1560 of 1920). It never covers her silhouette, the wheel hub or the blossom.
