The format decision

One shot: land, then paper, then land

Recommended

Continuous camera, land becomes map

The story is one physical journey (road, gate, records, road), so a single camera move fits it. With no cut there is no natural point to swipe away. The 3D land (0–18 s and 42.7–55.6 s) is new, path-traced and clearly better than v1. In between, the camera looks straight down, the survey lines become map ink, and we are in the paper scenes, which stay close to v1 because they already work.

Chapters with match-cuts

Easier to render, but every cut is an exit point in the first 10 seconds, and it reads like a template explainer. Rejected.

Rebuild the papers in 3D too

Possible (a lamp-lit desk in Cycles), but v1's paper scenes already read as 3D, and rebuilding them would roughly double render time and cost for little gain. Kept as v1, polished.

Subtitles

Odia hero word, English label, nothing else

Your instinct is right. Odia is the hero (it is who the page is for), and a small gold English line under each word keeps non-Odia viewers, NRIs and search with us. Only the 12 or so words that carry the advice get an overlay; the rest of the text lives inside the scene. One accent term per line is set in gold italic (Right of Way, Bhulekh Map). Figures use lining numerals, never old-style. Everything stays inside the safe zone, clear of Instagram's top bar and caption area.

Music and sound

A score cut to the picture

  • Mood: a classic, investigative feel, closer to a premium documentary than to a trending sound. Felt piano motif in D minor, a cello drone and a soft ticking pulse around 92 BPM ("the clock before the advance").
  • Hits: a deep braam and the metal clang at 6.4 s, a felt thump when the seal lands at 39.6 s. The music drops to a single held cello note under "ସାବଧାନ୍".
  • Resolve: at 42.7 s the harmony turns to D major as the gate opens, with strings rising into the end card and a short two-note logo sting.
  • Sound design: wind in the palms, birds, road dust, chain rattle, board creak; indoors a wall clock, a ceiling fan, paper slides, pen scratch, the stamp.
  • Mix: voice first. Music ducks about 12 LU under the voice; the master is −14 LUFS with true peak at −1.5 dB. As before, I'll also deliver a voice-and-effects version without music.

The score is played on sampled acoustic instruments (FluidSynth with the MuseScore General soundfont, confirmed installable here) instead of v1's synthesised tones, so the piano and strings sound real.

Production

How it gets made

  • The land (0–18 s, 42.7–55.6 s) is rendered in Blender Cycles. The paper scenes (18–42.7 s) are re-rendered from the v1 code with the polish above, which is quick and cheap. Odia text inside the 3D world (the board) is typeset with proper Odia shaping and printed onto the object.
  • The hand-offs are matched: the last 3D frame looks straight down on the same layout as the first map frame, so the land drains into cream paper along the survey lines, and the reverse happens at 42.7 s.
  • Before the full render: test stills at the middle of every phrase and at every transition, a pop scan between consecutive frames, and a check that every word stays inside the safe zone.
  • Render time: {{RENDER}}. I'll split it across 10–12 helper sessions as agreed, then join the chunks and check every seam for pops.
  • Delivered as before: full mix, a voice-and-effects version, voice only, and a cover image (the board frame).
